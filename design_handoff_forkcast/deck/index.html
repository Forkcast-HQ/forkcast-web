The problem

01 · The problem

The gap

Tracking apps

Calorie logging is manual, after the fact, and abandoned within weeks. The data lives apart from the decision it should inform — what to eat next.

Ordering apps

Delivery platforms optimize for cravings and basket size. Restaurant nutrition, when shown at all, is a PDF-grade afterthought with no link to the diner’s goal.

The product

02 · The product

The loop

  • A metabolic profile sets a personal daily budget — Mifflin-St Jeor, not a generic 2,000
  • Every dish nearby is scored against what remains of the day
  • Ordering is logging — the meal posts automatically when the kitchen accepts
02 · The product

The Fit Score

Calorie fit

A dish is scored against the diner’s remaining budget for the day, not a daily total — the same bowl scores differently at noon and at nine.

Protein density

Weighted by goal — cutting, maintaining or building each set their own protein target, per sports-nutrition guidance.

Guardrails

Sodium, sugar and fiber, plus personal allergy and condition flags. Sponsored placement can never move a score.

Business model

03 · Business model

Revenue

Order take rate

A percentage of each order placed through Forkcast, with delivery brokered to courier partners.

Forkcast Pay

Direct settlement between diner and restaurant — processing margin replaces the POS middleman.

Menu verification

A partner subscription for verified nutrition: provenance, review dates and the corrections workflow.

Partnerships

Gyms fund member perks that route members to partner restaurants — acquisition on both sides.

03 · Business model

Trust by design

  • Nutrition values are estimates and say so — every figure carries a source and a ± range
  • Allergen flags are never guaranteed; sponsored placement can never move a ranking
  • Pilot metrics are defined before launch — no claim ships without a recorded measurement
